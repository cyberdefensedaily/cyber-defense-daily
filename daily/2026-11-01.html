FBI Attributes $6B Binance Theft to North Korea as Federal Breaches Mount

Record crypto heist, DHS breach, and chained Ivanti exploits mark another severe week in cybersecurity.

FBI Confirms North Korea Behind Record $6.3 Billion Binance Theft

The FBI officially attributed the theft of more than $6 billion in cryptocurrency from Binance to North Korea's TraderTraitor hacking group on October 31. The attack occurred on October 29 and is believed to be the largest cryptocurrency heist on record. Binance resumed withdrawals in phases on November 1, with customers withdrawing $3.7 billion within hours, though the company has not confirmed recovery of the stolen assets. [1]

Homeland Security Breach Exposes Data on 40,000+ Personnel

A hacker obtained names, email addresses, phone numbers, and business contact information for more than 40,000 Department of Homeland Security employees and contractors, published online on October 28. The breach affected personnel across CBP, ICE, CISA, and other DHS agencies. DHS acknowledged a "potential breach" and said it is working to address the situation, but it remains unclear if the vulnerability has been fixed. [2]

CISA Adds Two More Ivanti Connect Secure Flaws to Exploited List

CISA added two additional Ivanti Connect Secure vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on October 30, bringing the total actively exploited flaws in the VPN appliance to three. Attackers are chaining CVE-2026-104935, CVE-2026-111357, and CVE-2026-118527 to gain unauthenticated root access since at least October 1. Federal agencies must patch CVE-2026-111357 by November 2. [3]

Florida Discloses Breach Affecting 4.9 Million Financial Aid Applicants

Florida's Department of Education discovered unauthorized access to its Student Financial Aid website on October 19, potentially exposing Social Security numbers and personal information of 4.9 million people. The department has not disclosed the attack method or whether stolen credentials or software vulnerabilities were involved. The breach adds to a wave of education sector compromises, including a separate incident at ed-tech provider Frontline affecting 50 million students. [4]
