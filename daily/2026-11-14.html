DOJ seizes $1.2B in North Korean crypto theft; critical exploits patched

Federal action on Binance hack leads major security disclosures from vendors

DOJ moves to seize $1.2 billion from Binance hack, disrupts deepfake scheme

The Justice Department announced Thursday it received court authorization to seize more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance in October, the largest crypto heist on record at roughly $6.3 billion total. The FBI attributed the theft to TraderTraitor, a North Korean military hacking operation. Separately, federal agents seized cloud infrastructure used in FaceStealer, a North Korean campaign that used recorded videos and AI-generated deepfakes to steal more than $400 million in cryptocurrency over 18 months. [1]

Xen Orchestra authentication bypass under active exploitation

Vates confirmed November 10 that attackers are actively exploiting VSA-2026-062, a critical authentication bypass in Xen Orchestra that allows unauthenticated access with admin privileges using a crafted empty-object cookie. The flaw can lead to arbitrary code execution on managed pools and full root takeover of XCP-ng virtualization nodes. Patches released in versions 6.9.2 and 6.10.1; no CVE has been assigned. [2]

Google patches eighth Chrome zero-day of 2026

Google patched CVE-2026-125350, a use-after-free vulnerability in Chrome's XML component, on November 9 after confirming active exploitation. The flaw was reported anonymously November 6, giving Google three days to ship the fix in Chrome 157.0.8103.50. CISA added the vulnerability to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog November 10 with a November 24 remediation deadline for federal agencies. [3]

Microsoft ships 1,240 patches including exploited Outlook zero-day

Microsoft's November 2026 Patch Tuesday addressed 1,240 vulnerabilities including CVE-2026-124194, an Outlook spoofing flaw under active exploitation that can leak NTLM authentication data. CISA added the vulnerability to its KEV catalog November 10. The release included 64 critical-severity flaws, among them remote code execution bugs in Microsoft Office and Windows components. [4]

Federal agencies warn of attacks on industrial refrigeration systems

CISA and the FBI warned November 5 of an ongoing cyber threat targeting internet-connected refrigeration and cold chain monitoring systems in grocery stores, cold storage facilities, U.S. military bases, and at least one blood bank. The joint statement said the attacks are affecting food, agriculture, transportation, and healthcare sectors. Security researchers have broadly attributed the campaign to Iran, though the government has not officially assigned attribution. [5]
