Critical PHP Flaws, GitLab RCE, and Certificate Authority Collapse Mark October's Cybersecurity Crisis

Week of emergency patches exposes decade-old code vulnerabilities and catastrophic cryptographic failures.

PHP2Shell vulnerabilities trigger emergency patching across the web

Two critical PHP vulnerabilities disclosed October 21 allow unauthenticated remote code execution on servers running the language that powers a substantial portion of websites. CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619 stem from memory corruption and stale global state issues, earning CVSS scores of 9.2. The NSA advised organizations to immediately update or take systems offline. Exploitation attempts began within hours of disclosure, with over 60,000 suspicious requests blocked by one platform alone. [1]

GitLab patches maximum-severity RCE flaw affecting 12 years of releases

GitLab released emergency fixes October 21 for CVE-2026-110264, a CVSS 10.0 vulnerability allowing unauthenticated remote code execution on self-managed servers. The integer overflow in regex compilation affects all versions back to GitLab 7.0 from 2014. No exploitation has been confirmed and the flaw is not on CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities list, but administrators who patched on October 12 remain vulnerable to this separate issue. [2]

eMudhra certificate authority collapses after private keys exposed as single-digit integers

Eight intermediate certificate authority private keys from Indian CA eMudhra were compromised due to catastrophic cryptographic weakness—one key was literally the number 4. Researchers recovered the P-384 keys in under a second from public certificates on October 17, potentially allowing attackers to issue fraudulent certificates for any website. Chrome, Firefox, Apple, and Microsoft blocked the certificates within days. eMudhra announced October 22 it will exit the global SSL/TLS business. [3]

Pentagon faces class-action lawsuit over data breach affecting 3 million

Service members and families sued the Pentagon this week over a Defense Manpower Data Center breach that allegedly left personal records of more than 3 million people exposed for nine months. The complaint alleges the Pentagon violated federal privacy laws despite internal Inspector General warnings about inadequate cybersecurity controls for 14 of 20 core security metrics. The breach is unrelated to a separate FBI personnel file cyberattack disclosed around the same time. [4]

French supermarket cyberattack spoils food, highlights critical infrastructure gaps

A cyberattack on French supermarket chains Intermarché and Coopérative U on October 12 compromised internet-connected freezers, causing refrigerators to defrost and spoiling millions in products across 150-200 Intermarché stores and nearly 100 Coopérative U outlets. Cybersecurity experts told Fox News the incident exposes similar vulnerabilities in U.S. critical infrastructure, citing recent coordinated cyberattacks on Minnesota water systems as evidence of threats to legacy operational technology systems.
