La Poste breach exposes 22M, DOJ seizes $1.2B from Binance hack, malware operation dismantled

Major data breach in France, North Korea cryptocurrency seizure, and international malware disruption lead the day

French postal subsidiary breach exposes 22 million records

Maileva, a subsidiary of France's La Poste, suffered a cyberattack exposing names and postal addresses of 22 million people who received correspondence through the platform. La Poste confirmed the theft on November 14, calling it one of France's largest data breaches. The stolen data includes information from customers of businesses and public organizations that use Maileva's mail services. [1]

DOJ seizes $1.2 billion stolen in North Korea's Binance hack

The Justice Department obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance in late October, part of more than $2.7 billion now frozen. The FBI attributed the theft of over $6 billion to North Korean military hackers known as TraderTraitor. Separately, DOJ disrupted the FaceStealer scheme, seizing infrastructure used to steal more than $400 million through deepfake video calls over 18 months. [2]

International operation dismantles Gootloader malware network

Law enforcement from nine countries seized 257 domains and 235 servers linked to Gootloader, CastleRAT, and StealC malware operations. Gootloader infected at least 25,000 computers worldwide since 2018 and enabled ransomware attacks causing roughly $28.8 million in U.S. losses. Authorities identified 6.4 million stolen credentials on seized infrastructure, which have been shared with breach notification services. [3]

Microsoft patches 1,235 vulnerabilities including exploited Outlook zero-day

Microsoft's November 10 Patch Tuesday addressed 1,235 security flaws, including 66 critical vulnerabilities and an actively exploited Outlook zero-day. CVE-2026-124194 allows attackers to steal NTLM authentication hashes through a spoofing vulnerability. The patch bundle also includes critical fixes for Azure OpenAI and Azure Managed Grafana privilege escalation flaws rated 9.8 on the CVSS scale. [4]

Expedia confirms supplier breach exposed traveler data

Expedia Group confirmed on November 12 that attackers accessed its systems through a compromised third-party data platform provider. The N0n extortion group claims to hold over one terabyte of data including loyalty program records, traveler profiles, and booking data affecting tens of millions of customers. Sample data leaked to the dark web contains hundreds of thousands of email addresses linked to hotel stays and loyalty details. [5]
