FBI removes contractor over PeopleSoft patch failure, Atlassian discloses critical flaw, FortiBleed still active

Unpatched systems drive three major security developments in October's first week

FBI removes Accenture contractor after patch failure enabled ShinyHunters breach

The FBI terminated an Accenture contractor for failing to apply a security patch to Oracle PeopleSoft, enabling ShinyHunters to breach the bureau's jobs portal and steal personal data from thousands of employees. The contractor missed a patch for CVE-2026-35273, a critical PeopleSoft flaw that allows unauthenticated remote code execution. According to Mandiant, ShinyHunters bypassed the FBI's web application firewall using URL encoding to reach the vulnerable endpoint. The stolen data includes details of current and former agents, applicants, and their families, with records containing counterintelligence assignments and home addresses. [1]

Atlassian warns of critical file-access flaw affecting eight self-hosted products

Atlassian disclosed CVE-2026-21589, a critical vulnerability rated 9.3 out of 10 that allows unauthenticated attackers to read specific files from the web application root directory in eight Data Center products including Jira, Confluence, and Bitbucket. Attackers must know exact file names and paths but require no login. All versions of the affected products are vulnerable. Cloud instances have been patched, but self-hosted deployments require immediate updates. No evidence of exploitation has been confirmed, and the flaw is not yet on CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities list. [2]

FortiBleed campaign still active, FBI warns of administrator lockouts

The FBI and Secret Service warned that the FortiBleed credential-harvesting campaign has compromised more than 86,644 Fortinet FortiGate firewalls across 194 countries, with some organizations now locked out of their own systems. The campaign, active since at least February 2026, systematically extracts configuration files from internet-facing devices and cracks credential hashes. Attackers are disabling legitimate admin accounts and changing passwords, making recovery require more than simple password resets. SOCRadar verified the working credentials represent roughly half of all internet-reachable FortiGate devices worldwide. [3]

Anthropic expands AI model access for verified security teams after disclosing 129,000 vulnerabilities

Anthropic announced it is expanding a program allowing vetted cybersecurity professionals to test its AI models with reduced safeguards, after its Project Glasswing initiative disclosed at least 129,000 verified software vulnerabilities between April and July 2026. The company reported an additional 5,500 verified vulnerabilities through separate open-source scanning efforts between April and October. The three-tier Cyber Verification Program integrates previously separate initiatives and gives security researchers access to advanced Claude models with fewer blocking classifiers. The timing follows industry debate over AI-assisted vulnerability discovery outpacing patch deployment. [4]
