FBI database breach, VMware zero-day, USDA disruption

Critical infrastructure and federal agencies hit by sustained cyber incidents

FBI's national crime database breached

The FBI is investigating an apparent breach of its National Crime Information Center, the nationwide database used by law enforcement agencies to share information on warrants, background checks, and criminal records. This marks the second breach of FBI systems in two months, following a September incident affecting employee data. The FBI has not publicly confirmed details of the intrusion or what data may have been accessed. [1]

China-linked group exploited VMware vCenter zero-day for two months

Broadcom patched CVE-2026-123890, a critical authentication bypass vulnerability in VMware vCenter Server rated 9.8 on CVSS, on November 19. Google's threat intelligence team reported that a suspected Chinese espionage group called ARMORY CASTLE exploited the flaw as a zero-day beginning in early September 2026, more than two months before disclosure. CISA added the vulnerability to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on November 20 with a November 23 remediation deadline for federal agencies. [2]

U.S. Department of Agriculture confirms cyberattack

The USDA confirmed a cybersecurity incident affecting its Farm Production and Conservation mission systems, including Geographic Information System mapping, payment processing, and contracting tools. According to sources familiar with the matter, the department shut down affected systems after detecting unauthorized access, disrupting farmer-payment services. The incident is in its fifth day as of November 20, with USDA Under Secretary Richard Fordyce confirming it as a cybersecurity incident. [3]

GAO report examines AI's dual role in cyber offense and defense

The Government Accountability Office published report GAO-27-108458 on November 20, examining how artificial intelligence accelerates offensive cyber operations while also transforming federal defense capabilities. The report outlines 16 policy options for federal and research computing, including establishing objective testing benchmarks for cybersecurity guidance and lifecycle governance to secure AI models against data poisoning. [4]

Commerce restricts remote access to U.S. AI chips

The Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security announced a rule on November 16 restricting remote access to advanced American AI chips and servers. The regulation closes a loophole that allowed adversarial governments, particularly China, to access the technology through data centers abroad without direct physical possession of the hardware. [5]
