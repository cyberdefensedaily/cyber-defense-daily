Optimizely supply chain attack hits 178 sites, Apache Axis flaw under active exploit

Major supply chain breach and critical library vulnerability top weekend cyber developments.

Optimizely supply chain attack compromised 178 organizations

Attackers exploited a vulnerability in Optimizely Web Experimentation on November 16 to inject malicious JavaScript into customer projects, delivering crypto wallet stealers and card skimmers to visitors of BBC, CNN, Zoom, HSBC and 174 other organizations. Security researcher Scott Helme identified 188 infected scripts; Optimizely confirmed the incident early November 17 and said it has fixed the vulnerability and removed the malicious code. Researchers traced approximately $7,000 to attacker-controlled wallets, though some were active before the attack. [1]

Critical Apache Axis flaw exploited against Confluence and N-able

CVE-2026-90436, a critical unsafe reflection vulnerability in end-of-life Apache Axis 1.x library, is under active exploitation against Atlassian Confluence Data Center and N-able N-central. Rapid7 detected exploitation against Confluence on November 13; N-able separately reported attacks the same day strongly correlated to the flaw. Both products are vulnerable only when non-default features are enabled, and Apache has no plans to release a fix for the end-of-life library. [2]

TSMC confirms cloud provider breach exposed employee data

Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company confirmed a data breach at an unnamed cloud provider that exposed information about current and former employees. A hacker advertised 6.9 GB of allegedly stolen employee data on a cybercrime forum, while the WhiteArmor extortion group claimed to have stolen approximately 100GB and demanded $5 million. TSMC stated its own systems and customer data were unaffected. [3]

Infoblox confirms vendor breach accessed company information

Cybersecurity giant Infoblox confirmed a security incident on November 16 involving unauthorized access to company information through a technology vendor that provides SharePoint data migration services. Two sources told TechCrunch that sensitive customer data was taken in the breach. Infoblox has not disclosed how many customers were affected or named the compromised vendor, which the company has since terminated. [4]
