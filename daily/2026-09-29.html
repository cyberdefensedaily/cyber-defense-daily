Pentagon breach hits 3 million, Citrix zero-days exploited, Apple patches targeted attack flaw

Major breach, critical infrastructure flaws, and a sophisticated zero-day mark the weekend

Pentagon data breach exposed 3 million military-linked personnel

The Defense Manpower Data Center confirmed unauthorized users accessed an information system between October 2025 and July 2026, exposing unencrypted data on 2.76 million living and 294,000 deceased individuals. The intrusion exploited a file-sharing vulnerability discovered July 16; exposed records included names, Social Security numbers, dates of birth, and military personnel data. DMDC patched the flaw immediately after discovery and initiated incident response procedures. [1]

Citrix patches two NetScaler zero-days exploited in the wild

Citrix released emergency fixes for CVE-2026-88771 and CVE-2026-88772, both rated CVSS 9.5, after confirming global exploitation. CVE-2026-88771 allows unauthenticated remote command execution on default configurations; CVE-2026-88772 is a memory overflow enabling RCE or denial of service when DTLS is enabled. CISA added both to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog and ordered federal agencies to patch by September 30. [2]

Apple patches CoreGraphics zero-day used in sophisticated targeted attack

Apple released iOS 26.7.1 and macOS updates to fix CVE-2026-86950, an out-of-bounds write in CoreGraphics exploited against specific individuals. The flaw, reported by Meta Product Security, allows arbitrary code execution when processing maliciously crafted files. Apple described the attack as extremely sophisticated but provided no additional details on targets or attackers. [3]

Bitget confirms $388 million crypto theft via third-party security flaw

Cryptocurrency exchange Bitget disclosed that attackers exploited a vulnerability in a third-party security product to obtain high-level internal credentials and issue fraudulent withdrawal commands on September 24. Private keys were not compromised, but the incident represents 2026's largest crypto hack. Bitget suspects North Korean actors and has frozen some stolen assets; the exchange recorded $463 million in customer outflows following the breach. [4]
