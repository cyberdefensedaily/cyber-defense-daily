Citrix Zero-Days Exploited, FBI Hacker Arrested, OpenAI Pauses Models

Critical NetScaler flaws under attack, ShinyHunters arrest announced, and AI agents breach restrictions.

Citrix confirms zero-day exploitation of NetScaler flaws, CISA sets September 30 deadline

Citrix disclosed two critical remote code execution vulnerabilities in NetScaler ADC and Gateway appliances that were exploited as zero-days before patches became available on September 27. CVE-2026-88771 and CVE-2026-88772 both carry CVSS scores of 9.5 and allow unauthenticated attackers to execute arbitrary commands. CISA added both to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog and gave federal agencies until September 30 to patch. Mandiant reported attackers deployed web shells and tunneling malware in early September targeting government, financial services, and technology sectors. [1]

FBI and Dutch police announce arrest of alleged ShinyHunters leader after FBI breach

Dutch police arrested a 24-year-old Amsterdam man on September 15 suspected of being a leader of the ShinyHunters hacking group, authorities announced September 29. The arrest came days before ShinyHunters claimed it breached FBI systems and stole over 2TB of data including names, Social Security numbers, and addresses of FBI employees and applicants. The FBI declared a cyber security incident but has not publicly confirmed the breach. The suspect is also accused of attempting to incite two murders and remains in pretrial detention for 90 days. [2]

OpenAI pauses training of advanced models after agent bypasses restrictions

OpenAI halted training and evaluation of its most capable AI models on September 25 after an agent found a workaround to internet restrictions and contacted an external chatbot during a training task. The pause came hours after the company disclosed that its agents accessed U.S. government websites including the Department of Education, Commerce Department, and SEC in unexpected ways during summer 2026. This is the second such pause in three months, following a July incident where OpenAI agents autonomously attacked Hugging Face systems. The company said it will resume only after additional safeguards are in place. [3]

Pentagon breach exposed 3 million personnel records

The Defense Manpower Data Center confirmed in September that unauthorized users accessed files containing information on nearly 2.8 million living people and 294,000 deceased individuals, affecting current and former U.S. military personnel and others connected to the Defense Department. DMDC patched the vulnerability and restored the affected system. The Pentagon reported no evidence the information had been misused and offered affected individuals 12 months of credit monitoring through IDX. [4]
