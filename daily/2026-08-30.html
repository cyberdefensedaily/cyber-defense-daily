Boston Scientific Attack Disrupts Global Operations; PaperCut Zero-Day Exploited

Medical device maker suffers ongoing disruption while print software flaw sees active exploitation.

Boston Scientific Cyberattack Disrupts Manufacturing and Shipping Globally

Medical device maker Boston Scientific disclosed August 26 that a cyberattack detected the previous day is disrupting its global operations, including its ability to process and ship customer orders. The company has not provided a timeline for restoration and said it has not yet determined whether the incident will have a material financial impact. No ransomware group has claimed responsibility. The attack follows similar incidents at Abbott Laboratories, Medtronic, and Stryker in 2026. [1]

PaperCut Warns All Versions Under Active Zero-Day Attack

PaperCut alerted customers August 28 that attackers are actively exploiting a vulnerability affecting all versions of its PaperCut NG and MF print management software in zero-day attacks. The company released emergency patches for versions 25 and 26 and confirmed it is aware of customer incidents. PaperCut said it is treating the matter with the highest priority but did not disclose technical details of the flaw. [2]

Australian Authorities Charge Two Over TeamPCP Supply Chain Attacks

Australian authorities charged two men August 27 accused of leading TeamPCP, a cybercrime group linked to supply chain attacks that potentially compromised more than 1,000 organizations and exposed 500,000 credentials. The investigation found the group's attacks drove global cleanup costs into the hundreds of millions of dollars. The arrests mark a significant enforcement action against a threat group that targeted third-party vendors to gain access to downstream victims. [3]

Pentagon Weighs Standalone Cyber Military Service as CYBERCOM Restructures

A Congressional Research Service report published August 19 examines arguments for and against establishing a dedicated military service for cyber operations as the Pentagon simultaneously implements CYBERCOM 2.0 reforms. The Senate Armed Services Committee narrowly rejected an amendment to create a Cyber Force in a 14-13 vote, with lawmakers citing the need to await a National Academies feasibility study. The FY2027 defense budget request allocates $7.7 billion to cyberspace operations. [4]
