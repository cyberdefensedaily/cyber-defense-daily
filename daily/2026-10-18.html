ConnectWise breach hits 173 Microsoft 365 tenants; Microsoft patches record 1,411 flaws

Supply-chain attack and mass vulnerability disclosure dominate the week's cyber news

ConnectWise Cloud Backup breach compromises 173 Microsoft 365 tenants

Attackers compromised ConnectWise Cloud Backup credentials and used them to access at least 173 Microsoft 365 tenants between October 14-17, according to security firm Huntress. ConnectWise shut down the backup service on October 17 after discovering unauthorized access to application identities that bypassed multifactor authentication. The company says no evidence indicates backup archives were accessed, but the scope of data theft remains unclear. [1]

Microsoft's October Patch Tuesday addresses record 1,411 vulnerabilities, one actively exploited

Microsoft released security updates for 1,411 flaws in its October 2026 Patch Tuesday, the highest count on record. One zero-day, CVE-2026-96951 in Windows Shell, is being actively exploited to bypass Mark of the Web protections. CISA added the flaw to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on October 13 with a remediation deadline of October 16. [2]

CISA flags four newly exploited flaws in Palo Alto, Cisco, and Ahsay products

CISA added four vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on October 14 based on active exploitation evidence. The flaws include CVE-2026-0322 in Palo Alto Networks PAN-OS, CVE-2026-76519 in Cisco Secure Email Gateway, and two vulnerabilities in Ahsay AhsayCBS backup software. Federal agencies had until October 17 to patch the vulnerabilities. [3]

Cyberattack on European grocery stores exploited refrigeration systems

Attackers exploited unpatched Danfoss refrigeration firmware at over 400 European grocery stores starting October 12, remotely converting freezers into heating units that reached 50°C. France's cybersecurity authority warned on October 16 that attacks were likely still underway. Danfoss confirmed the compromised systems were running outdated firmware and has urged customers to implement network segmentation and firmware updates. [4]

Pentagon cyber chief says demand for operations far exceeds supply

Assistant Secretary of Defense for Cyber Policy Katie Sutton said on September 23 that the Pentagon now receives far more requests for cyber operations than its forces can fulfill. Sutton said her single priority is expanding cyber options available to the president and defense secretary. A forthcoming DoD cyber strategy will focus on delivering high-impact offensive outcomes rather than persistent engagement operations. [5]
