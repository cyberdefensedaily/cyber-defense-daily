French Supermarket Hack, China Infrastructure Threat, SonicWall Under Active Attack

Refrigeration attack spoils food across hundreds of stores as critical infrastructure warnings mount

Hackers Force French Supermarket Refrigerators to Overheat, Sparking Fires

Attackers remotely triggered forced defrost cycles overnight October 11-12 in hundreds of French supermarkets, pushing refrigerators to 50°C and spoiling fresh and frozen food across at least 300 Intermarché and Netto stores, 100 Coopérative U stores, and 80 Carrefour locations. France's cybersecurity agency ANSSI traced the attack to vulnerabilities in Danfoss refrigeration controllers running outdated firmware. Paris prosecutors confirmed fires were sparked at several stores, with initial losses estimated at hundreds of thousands of euros per location and total damage expected to reach millions. [1]

CISA Sets October 11 Deadline for China-Linked Flax Typhoon Exploits

CISA added five vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog October 8 after confirming active exploitation by Flax Typhoon, a China-linked threat actor now formally attributed to Beijing contractor Integrity Technology Group. The flaws—spanning ProFTPD, ONLYOFFICE, Apache Struts, Strapi, and ISC BIND, some dating to 2015—require federal agencies to patch or discontinue use by October 11. The FBI simultaneously seized seven domains used for the group's MicroScan vulnerability scanner and FishHub phishing tools, which U.S. officials say are being used to preposition within critical infrastructure networks for future disruption. [2]

SonicWall Critical Flaw Under Active Exploitation Days After Patch

Threat actors began exploiting CVE-2026-102255, a CVSS 10.0 pre-authentication server-side request forgery flaw in SonicWall SMA 1000 appliances, within two days of SonicWall's October 6 patch release. Security firms Previdian and Defused Cyber detected 135 exploitation attempts by October 12 from two U.S.-based IP addresses targeting the WorkPlace interface to reach internal CouchDB services. SonicWall has not yet confirmed active exploitation in its advisory, and no successful compromises have been publicly verified. [3]

Low-Cost Android Phones Ship With Preinstalled Malware

Low-cost Android smartphones built on MediaTek platforms across several brands have been shipped with malicious firmware that covertly installs apps, conducts ad fraud, and turns devices into residential proxies, according to Bitdefender research published October 13. The activity, tracked as Midnight Mimosa, is baked into device firmware and allows bad actors to maintain persistent access. Bitdefender has not disclosed which specific device brands or models are affected. [4]
