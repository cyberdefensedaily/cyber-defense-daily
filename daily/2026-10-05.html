Pentagon Breach, ShinyHunters Arrest, Citrix Zero-Day

DMDC breach went undetected nine months; Citrix patches exploited vulnerability; hacker detained in Jordan.

Pentagon personnel breach went undetected for nine months

<cite index="12-3">The Defense Manpower Data Center disclosed unauthorized access to a file-sharing system between October 2025 and July 16, 2026, when the vulnerability was discovered.</cite> <cite index="12-4">The files contained unencrypted personal information, including Social Security numbers and, depending on the individual, names, birth dates, contact information and military occupational specialties.</cite> Three million people may have been affected. <cite index="12-2">Cybersecurity experts say months of unauthorized access to highly sensitive data going undetected is the real warning.</cite> [1]

Citrix patches NetScaler zero-day exploited in attacks

<cite index="29-2">Citrix disclosed a high-severity memory overflow vulnerability (CVE-2026-88779) affecting Citrix NetScaler ADC and Citrix NetScaler Gateway deployments configured as either a SAML Service Provider or SAML Identity Provider.</cite> <cite index="29-3">Successful exploitation can cause a denial of service condition, potentially disrupting authentication services and remote access functionality.</cite> <cite index="29-5,29-6">Active exploitation has been reported, and CISA has added CVE-2026-88779 to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities Catalog.</cite> [2]

ShinyHunters suspect detained in Jordan, reportedly aiding FBI

<cite index="50-3,50-4">A suspected member of the ShinyHunters digital extortion group, who goes by the online alias "Rey," has been allegedly detained by authorities in Jordan, with sources saying he was brought into custody on September 29, 2026, and is cooperating with the FBI.</cite> <cite index="49-3">Jordanian authorities detained Rey, identified as Saif al-Din Khader.</cite> <cite index="49-4">Two sources familiar with the arrest told Reuters that Khader is now helping the FBI and international law enforcement agencies locate other group members.</cite> [3]

China-linked hackers impersonated officials to target AI policy experts

<cite index="58-3">A China-aligned cyber espionage group targeted U.S. artificial intelligence policy experts through phishing emails that impersonated prominent officials, economists and an employee of AI company Anthropic, according to research released Thursday by Proofpoint.</cite> <cite index="58-4,58-5">The group began a campaign in July by impersonating a former White House technology official and an economist, with emails inviting recipients to join a supposed AI policy advisory committee or contribute to a report on AI export controls and supply chains.</cite> <cite index="58-6,58-7">In February the same group impersonated a senior Anthropic employee to ask an AI policy analyst at a U.S. think tank for feedback on the military's use of Claude AI models.</cite> [4]
