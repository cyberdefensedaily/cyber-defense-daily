PHP emergency patches, AI supply chains compromised, and SafePay ransomware hits 600

Critical vulnerabilities, supply-chain attacks, and ransomware dominate this week's threat landscape.

PHP2Shell vulnerabilities leaked before patches, governments respond

Two critical remote code execution flaws in PHP—CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619—leaked publicly on October 20 before patches were available, affecting PHP versions 5.4 through 8.5. PHP released emergency fixes October 21, but Amazon detected exploitation attempts dating to October 17, four days before disclosure. Governments including Sri Lanka issued urgent directives to state institutions to patch or isolate vulnerable systems. [1]

LiteLLM supply-chain attack delivered credential stealer via compromised release

Attackers compromised LiteLLM's release pipeline on October 21 and published malicious version 1.107.0 to PyPI and Docker registries containing credential-stealing malware targeting cloud, AI, and developer secrets. The compromised packages were available for roughly two hours before removal. Subsequent investigation revealed attackers gained initial access October 10 using a master key accidentally committed in May and never revoked. [2]

Railway warns users to rotate secrets after environment variable breach

Developer platform Railway disclosed October 21 that attackers may have accessed encrypted environment variables and obtained decrypted contents of some. The company urged all users to rotate API keys, cloud credentials, and other secrets stored as environment variables. Railway did not specify how many users were affected or how attackers gained access. [3]

Zeabur shuts down after breach exposed 3.2 million environment variables

Cloud platform Zeabur announced permanent shutdown following an October 14-15 breach that exposed 3.2 million environment variable records and data for 321,810 accounts. Attackers used leaked internal credentials stored in plaintext to compromise shared clusters and dedicated servers. The company acknowledged the credentials were not rotated after an earlier August breach and admin interfaces remained publicly accessible. [4]

CISA warns SafePay ransomware hit 600 critical infrastructure organizations

CISA, FBI, and international partners issued a joint advisory October 20 warning that SafePay ransomware has compromised at least 600 organizations across multiple critical infrastructure sectors since August 2024. The group targets organizations in North America, South America, Europe, and Australia through VPN exploitation, credential theft, and phishing. SafePay actors perform double extortion and deliberately delete backups to obstruct recovery. [5]
