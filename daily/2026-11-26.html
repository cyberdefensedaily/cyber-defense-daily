Federal agencies face breaches, three-day patch mandate, and intelligence system intrusions

Multiple U.S. agencies hit by cyber incidents as new patching requirements take effect.

FBI's national crime database targeted in suspected breach

The FBI is investigating an apparent breach of its National Crime Information Center, the database used by law enforcement nationwide to query warrants, missing persons, and stolen property. President Trump confirmed the incident on November 21, calling it "not good." The FBI has not publicly disclosed what was accessed, when the breach occurred, or who was responsible. Former FBI officials warn altered or missing data could endanger officers during traffic stops and other encounters. [1]

CIA and ODNI systems breached in unclassified intelligence network

The Central Intelligence Agency and Office of the Director of National Intelligence disclosed on November 24 that an unclassified open-source intelligence analysis system was breached. The National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency also reportedly experienced intrusions. The incidents follow a separate breach affecting the Treasury Department's Financial Crimes Enforcement Network, which took systems offline after unauthorized access to a limited subset of systems. [2]

CISA imposes three-day patch deadline under new risk-based directive

Federal agencies now face a three-day deadline to patch the highest-risk vulnerabilities under CISA's Binding Operational Directive 26-04, issued in June. The directive replaces flat patching timelines with a four-factor risk model based on asset exposure, Known Exploited Vulnerabilities status, exploit automation, and adversary control. CISA added two actively exploited flaws on November 23 with a November 26 deadline. The aggressive timeline reflects concerns that AI may accelerate the window between patch release and exploitation. [3]

AI agents linked to multiple breaches throughout November

November saw AI agents move from testing into real incidents, according to multiple breach disclosures. A China-linked group used an AI agent framework to target over 200 organizations in 11 days. Barracuda and other firms disclosed breaches traced to unrevoked cloud access keys issued to AI vendors for earlier projects. WorkOS attributed an internal sales application breach to an attack consistent with the ARTEX threat actor. The incidents prompted White House requests for regulatory leniency toward companies hit by AI-enabled attacks. [4]
