Critical PHP flaws force global site outages; Indian CA's weak keys compromised

NSA warns of PHP2Shell vulnerabilities as governments pull sites offline; eMudhra private keys trivially cracked.

NSA issues emergency warning as AI-discovered PHP flaws go under active exploitation

The National Security Agency advised organizations on October 21 to immediately patch or take offline systems running PHP after two critical vulnerabilities—CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, collectively called PHP2Shell—were disclosed with a 9.2 severity rating. Government websites in France, Denmark, Finland, and the Netherlands went dark as administrators raced to apply patches. Amazon detected exploitation attempts against its honeypots on October 17, four days before the vulnerabilities were publicly disclosed, and the flaws affect PHP versions going back years. [1]

Indian certificate authority eMudhra used the number 4 as a private key

Private keys for eight eMudhra intermediate certificate authority certificates were compromised due to catastrophically weak key generation—one key was literally the number 4, another was 56. The keys, created in April 2025, could be recovered from public certificates in under a second, potentially allowing attackers to forge trusted certificates for any website. Mozilla, Google, Apple, and Microsoft blocked the certificates between October 17-20 after a researcher reported the flaw, but non-browser software and embedded systems remain exposed until trust stores are manually updated. [2]

GitLab patches maximum-severity unauthenticated RCE flaw affecting 12 years of releases

GitLab released emergency fixes on October 21 for CVE-2026-110264, a critical vulnerability with a perfect 10.0 CVSS score that allows unauthenticated attackers to execute arbitrary code on self-managed GitLab servers. The flaw affects every version going back to GitLab 7.0, released in 2014. The security release also addresses eleven other vulnerabilities, including a high-severity LDAP account takeover issue. [3]

Ransomware groups exploit Palo Alto firewall flaw in ongoing campaign

Unit 42 observed active exploitation of PAN-OS vulnerability CVE-2026-0257 by ransomware operators including Qilin and Inc, with attackers pivoting directly to virtualization layers after compromise. The authentication bypass flaw in GlobalProtect allows unauthorized attackers to initiate VPN connections without credentials. Public reporting also attributes exploitation to threat actors deploying Settra ransomware, with attacks continuing as of October 20. [4]

Marines sue Pentagon over data breach, citing failure to meet cybersecurity standards

Two Marines filed a class-action lawsuit on October 22 against the Defense Department over a breach at the Defense Manpower Data Center that exposed personally identifiable information used by scammers. A post-breach Inspector General report found officials failed to demonstrate effective operations for 14 of 20 federal cybersecurity metrics. The lawsuit argues standard controls like vulnerability scanning and encryption of sensitive data could have prevented the breach. [5]
