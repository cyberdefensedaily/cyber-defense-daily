Utility breach via unprotected API; federal incident-reporting rule expected this month.

CenterPoint Energy confirms breach after hacker claims 7.5M customer records

CenterPoint Energy filed an SEC disclosure Monday confirming unauthorized access to customer information through an external-facing system after a threat actor posted claims to have stolen 7.49 million records. The attacker claimed the data was obtained by enumerating customer IDs via an unprotected public API lacking rate limiting or web application firewall protection, according to the hacker's post on a cybercrime forum September 12. The stolen data reportedly includes names, phone numbers, addresses, account numbers, billing amounts, and partial Social Security numbers. CenterPoint serves roughly 7 million customers across Indiana, Minnesota, Ohio, and Texas and says the breach has not impacted electricity or gas delivery. [1]

CISA expects final CIRCIA incident-reporting rule in September

The Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency now targets September 2026 for the final rule implementing the Cyber Incident Reporting for Critical Infrastructure Act, requiring covered entities across 16 critical infrastructure sectors to report substantial cyber incidents within 72 hours and ransomware payments within 24 hours. CISA published the proposed rule in April 2024 and missed the statutory October 2025 deadline after receiving extensive public feedback and holding additional stakeholder town halls in June 2026 attended by more than 1,200 industry representatives. Once effective, the rule will represent one of the most far-reaching U.S. cyber regulations ever implemented, applying to organizations ranging from electric utilities and water systems to hospitals and chemical facilities. [2]

Microsoft patches 972 vulnerabilities including critical SSTP VPN flaw

Microsoft's September 2026 Patch Tuesday addressed 972 vulnerabilities including CVE-2026-73009, a critical 9.8 CVSS remote code execution flaw in Windows Secure Socket Tunneling Protocol allowing unauthenticated attackers to execute code by sending a crafted packet to exposed SSTP VPN listeners. Organizations offering SSTP-based remote access VPN typically expose the vulnerable service directly to the internet on TCP/443, meaning exploitation requires no prior network access or credentials. The release also included two zero-day vulnerabilities already exploited in the wild: CVE-2026-81963 in Windows Update Stack and CVE-2026-85880 in Windows Advanced Local Procedure Call. The patch volume reflects Microsoft's deployment of AI-driven vulnerability scanning across its codebase. [3]

Senator Warner urges AI safety legislation by year-end

Senator Mark Warner is pushing Congress to pass federal AI safety legislation by the end of 2026, warning the window for action is closing as public anxiety over artificial intelligence reaches what he calls a tipping point. The House Judiciary Committee reconvened hearings on the Great American AI Act today, September 17, with the primary conflict centering on federal preemption. Tech industry representatives advocate for uniform national standards overriding state rules, while a coalition of 19 state attorneys general opposes preemption, arguing federal drafts establish weaker consumer protections than existing state legislation.
