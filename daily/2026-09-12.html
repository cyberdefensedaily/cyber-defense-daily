GitLab flaw under active exploit, AI agents hit 395 orgs via PaperCut

CISA warns of critical GitLab vulnerability as Russian actor deploys AI at scale.

CISA adds critical GitLab flaw to exploit catalog

CISA added CVE-2026-85706 to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on September 11 after confirming active exploitation. The path traversal flaw scores 10.0 on CVSS and allows unauthenticated attackers to read arbitrary files from GitLab servers through the repository commits API. Federal agencies must patch by September 14 under Binding Operational Directive 26-04, which also requires forensic triage. [1]

Russian actor uses AI agents to breach 395 organizations

A Russian-speaking threat actor deployed hundreds of autonomous AI agents to exploit two PaperCut vulnerabilities, compromising at least 440 instances across 395 organizations in 48 countries. GreyNoise tracked the operation to IP 45.142.193.132, observing AI agents that researched CVE-2026-81578 and CVE-2026-82078, developed exploits, and executed attacks with up to 200 concurrent threads. One U.S. high school went from initial access to full domain admin control in seven minutes. [2]

Microsoft patches 972 vulnerabilities including two zero-days

Microsoft's September Patch Tuesday addressed 972 vulnerabilities, including two actively exploited zero-days. CVE-2026-73009, a critical Windows SSTP RCE vulnerability with CVSS 9.8, allows unauthenticated attackers to execute code on internet-exposed VPN servers. The release included 113 critical flaws and 21 critical Office RCE bugs, 12 of which are exploitable via Preview Pane without user interaction. [3]

State Department offers $10M for Iranian cyber commander

The State Department's Rewards for Justice program announced a $10 million reward for information on Amir Yaryab, who leads Iran's IRGC Cyber Operations Command. Yaryab allegedly directs multiple IRGC-affiliated groups including CyberAv3ngers, which has targeted U.S. critical infrastructure including water systems, energy, and telecommunications. The bounty applies to individuals acting under foreign government direction who engage in malicious cyber activities violating the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act. [4]
