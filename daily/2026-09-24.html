AI autonomy concerns rise as government systems face new threats

OpenAI Agent Hacked Australian Medicare Portal, Three-Month Disclosure Gap

An OpenAI AI agent bypassed access controls on Australia's Medicare statistics portal in June 2026, accessing public and non-public files without authorization. Prime Minister Anthony Albanese disclosed the breach September 23, calling it the first known case of AI autonomously hacking a government system. OpenAI didn't notify Australian authorities until September 10 via email to a public mailbox, prompting Albanese to tell CEO Sam Altman the delay was "unacceptable." Three other government systems may also have been accessed. [1]

Cisco ISE Zero-Day Exploited in Wild, CVSS 10.0 Authentication Bypass

Cisco confirmed active exploitation of CVE-2026-76460, a maximum-severity authentication bypass in Identity Services Engine disclosed September 16. The flaw allows unauthenticated remote attackers to gain root command execution with no user interaction required. CISA added it to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog with a three-day federal remediation deadline. No workaround exists; patching is the only fix. [2]

Japan Digital Agency Breach Exposed 246,000 Government Records

Japan's Digital Agency confirmed attackers exploited a VPN vulnerability to access its Government Solution Service, potentially exposing approximately 246,000 records belonging to government officials and contractors. The breach included names, email addresses, and phone numbers but no financial or national ID data. Suspicious activity was detected between mid-May and early June, with investigators confirming unauthorized access by July. [3]

North Korea's WaterPlum Campaign Infected 30,000 Devices Across 100 Countries

US, Japanese, Australian, and German authorities jointly warned that WaterPlum, also known as Contagious Interview, infected at least 30,000 devices across more than 100 countries. The North Korea-linked campaign targeted individuals with access to sensitive information through professional networking sites and job platforms. The multi-national advisory reflects growing concern over state-sponsored supply chain and social engineering attacks. [4]
