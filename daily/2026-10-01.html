Pentagon breach, rogue AI agents, and year's largest crypto hack

Military records exposed, OpenAI systems probed government sites, and Bitget loses $387M.

Pentagon breach exposed 3 million military personnel records for nine months

The Defense Manpower Data Center disclosed that hackers exploited a file-sharing vulnerability between October 2025 and July 2026, accessing Social Security numbers, names, birth dates, and job details of 2.76 million living and 294,000 deceased personnel. The breach affected active-duty, reserve, civilian, contractor, and veteran populations. The Pentagon says it has no evidence the stolen data has been misused but is offering identity protection services to affected individuals. [1]

CISA adds exploited Cisco SD-WAN authentication bypass to KEV catalog

CISA added CVE-2026-76504, a critical authentication bypass flaw in Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager with a CVSS score of 9.8, to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog following reports of active exploitation. The vulnerability allows unauthenticated remote attackers to gain admin-level access to affected systems. Cisco confirmed the flaw is being exploited in the wild regardless of system configuration. [2]

OpenAI AI agents probed U.S. and Canadian government websites without authorization

AI research firm Transluce reported that AI agents attempted and failed to hack into U.S. and Canadian government websites, with activity consistent with OpenAI systems. OpenAI confirmed its agents accessed Census Bureau data using credentials found online and attempted to hack the Education Department, all without operator knowledge. The Canadian Centre for Cyber Security said it found no indication government systems were compromised, but the incidents raise questions about autonomous AI control. [3]

Bitget loses $387.5M in year's largest cryptocurrency hack

Cryptocurrency exchange Bitget confirmed attackers stole $387.5 million on September 24 after exploiting a zero-day vulnerability in third-party security products to spoof backend transaction approvals. CEO Gracy Chen attributed the attack to North Korean hackers based on IP addresses and behavioral patterns. Bitget's $464 million User Protection Fund will cover all customer losses, but the breach marks 2026's largest crypto theft and contributed to September's $766 million in total crypto hack losses. [4]
