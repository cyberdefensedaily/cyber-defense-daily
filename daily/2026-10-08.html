Critical Atlassian flaw exploited within hours, FBI removes contractor after breach

Fast exploitation cycles and third-party failures dominated the past 48 hours

Attackers exploit critical Atlassian flaw hours after public PoC

CVE-2026-21589, a critical unauthenticated file access vulnerability affecting eight Atlassian Data Center products including Jira and Confluence, came under active exploitation within two hours of watchTowr publishing technical details and proof-of-concept code on October 6. Previdian honeypots detected 158 exploitation attempts from 26 attacker IPs across eight countries by October 7. The flaw allows attackers to read files from application web roots without authentication using a double-colon traversal technique that bypasses path protections. [1]

FBI removes Accenture contractor after missed patch enabled ShinyHunters breach

The FBI terminated an Accenture contractor on October 6 after the contractor failed to apply a critical security patch for CVE-2026-35273 in Oracle PeopleSoft, allowing ShinyHunters to breach the FBI's job portal and steal personal data from thousands of bureau employees and applicants. Mandiant reported that ShinyHunters used a URL-encoding technique to bypass web application firewall rules protecting the vulnerable PeopleSoft Environment Management Hub endpoint. The FBI confirmed two ShinyHunters members have been arrested. [2]

CrowdStrike links China-based attacker to AI-powered South Korean bank breaches

CrowdStrike reported October 7 that a suspected 26-year-old China-based attacker used the open-source ARTEX AI penetration-testing tool alongside Anthropic's Claude Code to breach multiple South Korean financial institutions between late September and early October 2026, exposing data from 68,000 individuals. The attacker ran ARTEX primarily on DeepSeek v4.1-flash and other Chinese LLMs, and investigators found exposed server directories containing Claude Code session histories and Chinese-language prompts asking about marketplaces for stolen Korean data. South Korean authorities confirmed intrusions at seven financial institutions. [3]

CISA warns of increased ransomware targeting operational technology

The Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency issued a warning October 7 about a rise in ransomware attacks targeting operational technology environments in critical infrastructure sectors. CISA emphasized the need for enhanced defenses and incident response plans to mitigate risks of downtime and data loss in OT systems. [4]
