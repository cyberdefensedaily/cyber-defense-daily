FBI Database Breach, China's vCenter Zero-Day, and NATO's AI Defense Deal

Critical infrastructure compromised as federal systems face renewed scrutiny.

FBI's National Crime Database Breached

The FBI is investigating a suspected breach of the National Crime Information Center (NCIC), the nationwide database used by law enforcement to share warrants, missing persons records, and criminal history data. President Trump confirmed the incident Saturday, calling it "not good," though the FBI has not publicly confirmed details or attribution. This marks the second reported FBI breach in two months, following a September incident involving employee data. [1]

China-Linked Group Exploited VMware Zero-Day for Months

Broadcom patched CVE-2026-123890 on November 19, a critical VMware vCenter authentication bypass that China-linked espionage group Armory Castle exploited as a zero-day since early September. The vulnerability, with a CVSS score of 9.8, allowed unauthenticated attackers to gain administrator access to vCenter. Google's Mandiant documented multiple incidents involving custom malware including a Golang exploit tool called BONESAW. CISA added the flaw to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on November 20. [2]

OpenAI Gives NATO Access to Cyber Defense AI Models

OpenAI announced November 17 that NATO's Communications and Information Agency is using its Daybreak program for defensive cybersecurity work on Allied networks. NATO has used up to 300 billion input and output tokens per month for tasks including vulnerability investigation and software review. The arrangement extends to allied defenders including Ukraine's Ministry of Digital Transformation and European national cyber agencies, though neither party disclosed financial terms. [3]

USDA Confirms Multi-Day Cyberattack Disruption

The U.S. Department of Agriculture confirmed a cyberattack that forced the shutdown of internal applications including farmer payment tools and Geographic Information System mapping for at least five days. The department's Farm Production and Conservation mission sent notice Monday of a security breach affecting payments and contracting systems. The incident adds to a wave of federal agency breaches reported between September and November 2026. [4]
