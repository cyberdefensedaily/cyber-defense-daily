CISA strategy published, GitLab CVSS 10 patched, Pentagon breach lawsuit filed

Critical infrastructure defense strategy, maximum-severity GitLab RCE, and legal action over military data breach lead today

CISA releases 2030 strategy promising proactive defense

CISA published its CISA 2030 strategy on October 26, shifting from passive defense to an aggressive, anticipatory approach for critical infrastructure. The plan commits to reviewing core services within 60 days and prioritizing consequence-based risk analysis over all-hazards approaches. The strategy comes as the agency rebuilds from significant Trump administration budget cuts and workforce reductions. [1]

GitLab patches CVSS 10.0 unauthenticated RCE affecting 12 years of releases

GitLab released patches on October 21 for CVE-2026-110264, a maximum-severity integer overflow in its regular expression compiler that could allow unauthenticated remote code execution. The flaw affects all self-managed GitLab versions back to 7.0 from 2014. GitLab reports no evidence of exploitation, but the vulnerability requires no authentication and affects both Community and Enterprise editions. [2]

Pentagon sued over data breach exposing 3 million military records

Two Marines filed a class-action lawsuit on October 22 alleging the Defense Department violated the Privacy Act by failing to safeguard personal information in the Defense Manpower Data Center breach. The lawsuit claims DoD knew about cybersecurity flaws but intentionally disregarded its responsibilities. Unauthorized access ran from October 2025 to July 2026, exposing unencrypted Social Security numbers and military records. [3]

Node2Shell Brotli flaw triggers patching across Microsoft, Node.js, Python

CVE-2026-111497, a critical out-of-bounds write vulnerability in Google's Brotli compression library, prompted security updates from Microsoft, the Node.js project, and Python package maintainers on October 26. The flaw can lead to code execution and affects Node.js, .NET, PowerShell, and multiple Python packages. The vulnerability underscores risks in widely deployed compression libraries. [4]

KB Kookmin Bank confirms 2,091 additional victims in cyberattack

South Korea's KB Kookmin Bank announced on October 26 that an additional 2,091 people had their personal information leaked due to an external cyberattack. The disclosure represents an expansion of a previously reported breach at one of South Korea's largest banks. The bank has not disclosed the full scope of the incident or attribution. [5]
