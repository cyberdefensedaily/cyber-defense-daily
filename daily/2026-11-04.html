FBI attributes record crypto theft, DHS breach expands, China announces AI safety law

North Korea linked to $6.3B Binance hack as federal breach toll climbs and Beijing moves on regulation.

FBI formally attributes $6.3 billion Binance hack to North Korea

The FBI on November 1 linked North Korea's TraderTraitor group to the theft of over $6.3 billion from Binance, the largest cryptocurrency heist on record. Binance halted withdrawals and covered losses using company funds; roughly $2.85 billion was frozen on-chain by multiple providers. North Korea denied involvement on November 3, calling the FBI probe "a trite provocation." [1]

Department of Homeland Security breach exposes 40,000 employee records

A hacker published personnel data on over 40,000 DHS employees from a previously undisclosed breach of an Okta-based system. The leaked data includes names, phone numbers, and group access permissions for staff across ICE, CBP, HSI, and CISA. DHS confirmed it is investigating; the breach follows similar intrusions at the FBI and Pentagon this year. [2]

China announces AI safety rules covering full development lifecycle

China's National Development and Reform Commission announced on November 3 it will accelerate comprehensive AI legislation with safety standards covering data, computing power, and algorithms from development to deployment. The rules would apply to domestic firms including DeepSeek, Moonshot, and Alibaba, with a compliance deadline of February 2027. No specific regulatory text has been published. [3]

French blood banks thwart temperature-manipulation cyberattack

Three French blood storage sites detected and stopped a cyberattack on November 1 that attempted to raise freezer temperatures to 110°C, far above the normal -30°C. The French Blood Establishment confirmed no blood products or samples were compromised due to rapid detection. The attack follows similar refrigeration-targeting intrusions across European supermarkets. [4]
