CIA and ODNI hit by unclassified breach as leadership reshuffles continue

CIA and ODNI discover breach of open-source intelligence system

The CIA and Office of the Director of National Intelligence disclosed a breach affecting an open-source intelligence analysis system that exposed unclassified information. Officials confirmed no classified material or critical capabilities were compromised, though information from other government agencies may also have been exposed. The intrusion's scope, the specific data taken, and the attacker's identity remain undetermined. [1]

DNI Clayton named acting national cyber director amid tech outages

Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton will serve as acting national cyber director while Kirsten Davies, the Pentagon's chief information officer, awaits Senate confirmation for the permanent role. The move follows President Trump's dismissal of Sean Cairncross last week and comes after a series of tech disruptions affecting airlines, banks, and government systems including the FAA and Microsoft Outlook. [2]

Bell Canada suffers breach from 'agentic threat actor'

Bell disclosed that an agentic threat actor gained unauthorized access to internal systems on November 17, exposing customer data including names, addresses, phone numbers, account details, and payment information for Bell, Bell Aliant, Bell MTS, Virgin Plus, and Lucky Mobile customers. The company said it immediately contained the incident and removed the threat actor. A separate concurrent incident affected Bell MTS email service through a third-party provider. [3]

Critical NGINX HTTP/2 vulnerability disclosed

Rapid7 and F5 disclosed CVE-2026-123007, a critical integer underflow vulnerability in NGINX HTTP/2 that allows unauthenticated remote code execution under certain conditions. The vulnerability, with a CVSS score of 9.2, was disclosed to F5 on November 12 and patched by November 18. Rapid7 released unauthenticated vulnerability checks in its November 19 content release. [4]

CISA warns of critical Java vulnerability 'Java4Shell'

CISA issued an urgent alert for Oracle Java vulnerability CVE-2026-114938, a critical memory-corruption flaw in the Base64 decoder of the x86-64 HotSpot JVM. The vulnerability, known as Java4Shell, allows specially crafted Base64 input to crash the JVM and enable unauthenticated remote code execution. CISA strongly urged organizations to immediately apply Oracle's November 2026 security patch. [5]
