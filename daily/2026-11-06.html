North Korean Hackers Steal $6.3 Billion From Binance in Record Crypto Heist

Hackers believed to be from North Korea stole approximately $6.3 billion in digital assets from crypto exchange Binance by duping a DevOps engineer into downloading malware during a fake job interview. The attack, disclosed November 3, marks the largest cryptocurrency theft on record. Binance has frozen roughly $2.85 billion of the stolen funds and says it will cover all customer losses, though the breach exposed gaps in security tools that failed to detect the initial compromise. [1]

U.S. Warns of Global Cyberattacks Targeting Industrial Freezers at Military Bases, Supermarkets

CISA, the FBI, and other federal agencies issued a joint warning November 5 about an ongoing cyber threat targeting internet-connected refrigeration systems in food, healthcare, and transportation sectors. Attacks spoiled food at U.S. military commissaries in August and compromised cold storage at a French blood bank. Security researchers broadly attribute the campaign to Iran, though the government has not officially confirmed attribution. [2]

OpenSSH Authentication Bypass Exploited Within Hours of Disclosure

A decade-old OpenSSH vulnerability allowing attackers to bypass password authentication on Alpine Linux systems was exploited in the wild within nine hours of its November 5 disclosure. CVE-2026-123158, dubbed "sshole," affects Linux systems using musl libc without PAM and allows remote login to password-disabled accounts using oversized passwords. OpenSSH 10.6p2 patches the flaw, which threat intelligence firm Previdian confirmed was used to gain root access on honeypot systems. [3]

Hackers Publish Data on 41,000 Homeland Security Employees

A hacker group calling itself the Department of Peace published work contact details and internal group memberships for more than 41,000 DHS employees and contractors, including ICE and CBP agents. The breach, which occurred around mid-August and was disclosed October 28, exposed names, phone numbers, email addresses, and details of surveillance programs. DHS acknowledged the potential breach but has not confirmed whether the vulnerability has been fixed. [4]

Google Patches 216 Android Vulnerabilities Including Critical Zero-Click RCE

Google's November 2026 Android security update addresses 216 vulnerabilities, including a critical zero-click remote code execution flaw in the Framework component requiring no user interaction. The update also patches CVE-2026-58882, a critical denial-of-service vulnerability nicknamed "SMS of Death." Fixes are distributed across two security patch levels dated November 1 and November 5, covering Framework, System, Kernel, and vendor-specific components from Qualcomm, MediaTek, and others. [5]
