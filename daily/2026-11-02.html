FBI Confirms North Korea Behind Record Binance Heist; DHS Breach Exposes 41,000 Personnel Files

Biggest crypto theft, federal breach, and actively exploited Cisco and Ivanti flaws dominate weekend.

FBI Attributes $6.3 Billion Binance Hack to North Korea's TraderTraitor Group

<cite index="46-3">The FBI officially attributed the theft of more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance to TraderTraitor, a North Korea-aligned hacking group</cite>, making it the largest cryptocurrency heist in history. <cite index="47-5">The FBI says it has observed TraderTraitor converting stolen assets primarily into Ethereum (ETH), which it expects will be further laundered through decentralized finance (DeFi) platforms</cite>. <cite index="48-6,48-7">CEO Richard Teng said Binance had replenished approximately 95% of the affected assets and that Binance will absorb the financial impact of the stolen funds, not users</cite>. Hacker Publishes Personnel Data on 41,000 DHS Employees, Including Undercover Agents

<cite index="55-1">Personnel data on more than 41,000 Department of Homeland Security employees has been published online after a hacker stole reams of data in a previously undisclosed breach at the government department earlier this year</cite>. <cite index="56-6">A hacktivist group known as Department of Peace took credit for stealing the personnel files, which encompass roughly one-fifth of all agency staff</cite>. <cite index="58-5">A source familiar with the data breach confirmed that the information of some undercover personnel had been exposed</cite>. <cite index="55-7">It's unclear whether the vulnerability that allowed the breach has been fixed or if Homeland Security is aware of the data theft</cite>. [2]

Ivanti Patches Two More Zero-Days Chained in Connect Secure Attacks

<cite index="31-1,31-2">Ivanti has released patches for two more Connect Secure zero-days that attackers chained with a vulnerability the company disclosed last month, CVE-2026-111357 and CVE-2026-118527</cite>. <cite index="31-5,31-6">CVE-2026-111357, which Ivanti rates 8.8 on the CVSS scale, stems from the lack of authentication for a critical function, while CVE-2026-118527, with a CVSS score of 4.3, involves the generation of predictable identifiers</cite>. <cite index="31-9,31-10">CISA added the two newly disclosed vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on Friday, giving federal agencies until November 2 to address CVE-2026-111357 and until November 13 for CVE-2026-118527</cite>. [3]

Cisco Patches Actively Exploited SSL VPN Zero-Day in Firewall Products

<cite index="66-1,66-5">Cisco released fixes for an exploited vulnerability affecting its Secure Firewall ASA and FTD software, tracked as CVE-2026-76418 and assigned a CVSS score of 8.6</cite>. <cite index="67-3">Successful exploitation allows unauthenticated, remote attackers to trigger a denial-of-service condition, causing the device to reload unexpectedly</cite>. <cite index="66-8">Cisco's product security team learned in October that the flaw was being exploited in the wild</cite>. <cite index="2-11">Cisco confirmed that the vulnerability had been exploited in attacks before the security updates became available</cite>. [4]

Pacific Command Deploys Cyber Defense Forces to Hawaii Theater

<cite index="16-2,16-3">The Pentagon has shipped cyber operators from Maryland to Hawaii under a concept called Cyber Defense Forces Pacific, with personnel from the Defense Information Systems Agency Pacific providing a level of additional cybersecurity defense in the theater</cite>. <cite index="16-5,16-6">The personnel were drawn from the DoD's Cyber Defense Command, which is based in Fort Meade and falls under US Cyber Command and is responsible for defending the Pentagon's data networks</cite>. <cite index="16-7">Experts say that Beijing has been caught targeting the networks of military critical infrastructure installations in Guam in an effort to cripple a Washington response to action in the region</cite>.
