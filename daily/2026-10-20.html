Army pushes autonomous cyber agents as ConnectWise breach hits 173 tenants

Offensive AI, supply-chain access, and a seven-week zero-day dominated the weekend.

Army Cyber Command builds AI agents for offensive cyber operations

Documents obtained by The Intercept show Army Cyber Command is developing autonomous AI agents capable of navigating and neutralizing adversary computer systems. The command evaluated 65 vendor proposals during an October 6-8 assessment event and narrowed the field to five finalists. Task Force Lexington has already mapped AI work roles for an entire cyber protection team, according to Lt. Gen. Christopher Eubank. [1]

ConnectWise breach compromises 173 Microsoft 365 tenants via stolen app credential

Attackers used a compromised ConnectWise Cloud Backup API credential to authenticate into at least 173 Microsoft 365 tenants between October 13 and 15, according to Huntress and Microsoft. The stolen credential allowed access to Exchange, Teams, SharePoint, and Microsoft Graph without triggering MFA. ConnectWise disabled the affected app identities and suspended its Cloud Backup service on October 17, with no restoration timeline announced. [2]

Chinese APT exploited BeyondTrust zero-day for seven weeks before disclosure

China-linked group Armory Castle exploited CVE-2026-102764, a critical BeyondTrust Remote Support flaw, as a zero-day starting August 29, according to Google Threat Intelligence Group and Mandiant. BeyondTrust disclosed the vulnerability on October 15 and patched SaaS customers on October 9. CISA added the flaw to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on October 16. [3]

ETS breach exposes millions of California student records dating to 2006

An attacker used compromised credentials to access an Educational Testing Service SFTP server and copy personal records of millions of California public school students from May 22 through September 18. ETS discovered the breach on September 25 and notified the California Department of Education, which began notifying families on October 16. Exposed data includes names, birth dates, addresses, disability information, and assessment results, but no Social Security numbers or financial data. [4]

Microsoft patches record 1,413 vulnerabilities including exploited Windows Shell zero-day

Microsoft's October 2026 Patch Tuesday addressed 1,413 CVEs, 78 rated Critical, including CVE-2026-96951, a Windows Shell security feature bypass under active exploitation. A second zero-day, CVE-2026-108398, was publicly disclosed before patching and allows bypassing Windows Defender Application Control enforcement. CISA added the exploited flaw to its KEV catalog with an October 16 remediation deadline. [5]
