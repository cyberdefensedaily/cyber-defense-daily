China-nexus espionage, PHP exploitation, and AI-written kernel exploits

CISA adds VMware flaw to KEV catalog while active attacks target network infrastructure.

China-linked Warp Panda exploited VMware ESX zero-day to hide audit trails

<cite index="46-3,47-3">CISA added CVE-2026-41709 to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on October 28, after CrowdStrike reported China-nexus group Warp Panda exploited the VMware ESX logging flaw as a zero-day in early 2026 against U.S. legal sector organizations.</cite> <cite index="47-1,48-9">The flaw scores just 2.7 on CVSS but lets an attacker with admin access perform operations without leaving log entries.</cite> <cite index="48-7">Federal civilian agencies must patch by November 11, 2026.</cite> [1]

Palo Alto warns of active PHP2Shell exploitation against exposed management interfaces

<cite index="56-2,56-4">Palo Alto Networks on Wednesday warned that threat actors are exploiting the recently patched PHP2Shell vulnerabilities against publicly-accessible management interfaces, with active exploitation observed targeting internet-exposed Palo Alto Networks management interfaces.</cite> <cite index="56-5,56-6">The vulnerabilities, CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, carry CVSS scores of 9.2 and allow unauthenticated remote code execution; they were patched by the PHP Foundation on October 21.</cite> <cite index="59-5,59-6">CISA added both to its KEV catalog on October 26, confirming in-the-wild exploitation.</cite> [2]

Anthropic AI model wrote exploit for critical Linux kernel TCP flaw

<cite index="65-3,65-4,65-5">Anthropic says an unreleased Claude research model wrote a working exploit for CVE-2026-98432, a critical Linux kernel bug that lets a remote attacker run commands as root with no login; Anthropic's Frontier Red Team shared the finding on October 27, 2026, and the flaw is already patched upstream.</cite> <cite index="6-7,6-9">The kernel's own CVE team rates the TCP use-after-free 9.8 out of 10, and as of October 29, Ubuntu and RHEL have not released a fixed kernel.</cite> [3]

NIST releases post-quantum cryptography transition guidance

<cite index="3-3">The National Institute of Standards and Technology has released guidance on moving to post-quantum cryptography, giving federal agencies and technology providers a clearer path to quantum-resistant security.</cite> The guidance arrives as government agencies prepare for evolving federal cybersecurity requirements. No timeline for mandatory federal adoption has been specified in available reports. [4]
