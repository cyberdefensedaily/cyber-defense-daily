Revolut Breach, GitLab Zero-Day, EU Reporting Launch, Defense Cyber Market Doubles

Social engineering hits fintech, CISA warns on critical flaw, EU centralizes disclosure, military cyber spending surges.

Revolut Discloses Breach After Handing Customer Data to Impersonator

Fintech company Revolut confirmed September 14 that it shared customer data with a threat actor impersonating a government agency through a fraudulent legal request. The company disclosed the breach after an unauthorized party used a legitimate government email domain to request customer information. The number of affected customers and the scope of compromised data remain undisclosed. [1]

CISA Warns of Active Exploitation of GitLab Maximum-Severity Flaw

CISA issued an alert September 14 that threat actors are exploiting a critical path traversal vulnerability in GitLab allowing unauthenticated attackers to read arbitrary files from GitLab servers. The flaw carries a CVSS score of 10.0. GitLab patched the vulnerability, but exploitation began within 24 hours of public disclosure. [2]

EU Launches Single Reporting Platform for Cyber Resilience Act Disclosures

ENISA activated its centralized reporting platform September 14 for manufacturers to report actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents under the EU Cyber Resilience Act. The portal routes notifications to appropriate authorities and designated CSIRTs across member states. The system aims to reduce duplicate reporting and surface cross-product attack patterns that individual national reports might miss. [3]

Defense Cyber Warfare Market Projected to Double by 2031

A MarketsandMarkets report published September 14 projects the global cyber warfare market will grow from $14.99 billion in 2026 to $28.75 billion by 2031. The analysis cites rising attacks on military networks, greater reliance on connected platforms and cloud systems, and Western governments developing offensive cyber capabilities. In August 2026, President Trump authorized federal agencies to collaborate with private firms on offensive cyber operations against foreign threat actors. [4]
