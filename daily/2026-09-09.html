Adobe zero-day, N-able RCE, and CISA expands KEV list

Critical exploited flaws in e-commerce and RMM platforms drive emergency patching

Adobe patches StyleSmuggler zero-day exploited since September 4

Adobe released an emergency fix on September 7 for CVE-2026-75650, a CVSS 10.0 remote code execution flaw in Adobe Commerce and Magento Open Source. Security firm Sansec confirmed exploitation began September 4, three days before the patch, with attackers deploying Rust backdoors and PHP web shells on compromised e-commerce sites. At least one fully patched store was compromised. Adobe recommends merchants rotate encryption keys and all credentials those keys protected. [1]

N-able issues fourth hotfix in five weeks for exploited RCE flaw

N-able patched CVE-2026-86218, a CVSS 10.0 pre-authentication remote code execution vulnerability in N-central, on September 5 with Hotfix 4. The company's internal notice to customers stated the flaw has been exploited in the wild, though its public advisory said exploitation remains unconfirmed. This is the fourth critical N-central patch since early August, following a series of authentication bypass and RCE vulnerabilities. CISA added CVE-2026-86218 to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on September 9. [2]

CISA adds seven exploited vulnerabilities to KEV catalog

The U.S. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency added seven security flaws to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on September 2, including critical issues in SonicWall SMA 1000 Appliances and Sangoma Switchvox. CVE-2026-83548, a CVSS 10.0 server-side request forgery flaw in SonicWall, allows remote unauthenticated attackers to gain unauthorized access to sensitive functionality. Federal agencies must apply patches for most vulnerabilities by September 5, with additional time granted for two flaws until September 16. [3]

U.S. military cyber operations face integration challenges

Despite public statements from military leadership describing cyber effects integrated with kinetic operations in recent conflicts, offensive cyber operations remain difficult to fit into joint warfighting doctrine, according to a new FDD analysis. The report notes that for a decade, U.S. cyber forces have postured to disrupt threats below the level of armed conflict through persistent engagement, but integrating these capabilities with traditional military operations at scale continues to pose doctrinal and operational challenges. [4]
