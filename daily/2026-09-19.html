Check Point RCE flaw, NightmareStresser takedown, GitLab under active attack

Critical vulnerabilities and law enforcement disruption mark the week in cyber defense.

Check Point patches critical management server vulnerability

Check Point released patches for CVE-2026-91843, a critical stack-based buffer overflow allowing unauthenticated remote code execution with root privileges on Security Management and Log Servers. The flaw, rated 9.8 on CVSS, affects the login process before authentication completes. Check Point reports no evidence of exploitation in the wild, though the company has faced multiple zero-day attacks on its products in recent months. [1]

FBI seizes NightmareStresser DDoS-for-hire platform

The Justice Department seized domains of NightmareStresser, a DDoS-for-hire service operational since at least 2022, as part of Operation PowerOFF. Court documents indicate the platform enabled hundreds of thousands of attacks targeting educational institutions, government agencies, and gaming platforms. The DOJ did not announce arrests. A prior domain connected to NightmareStresser was seized in 2022. [2]

GitLab vulnerability actively exploited after CISA warning

CISA added CVE-2026-85706 to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog after confirming active exploitation of the maximum-severity GitLab flaw. The path traversal bug, scored 10.0 on CVSS, allows unauthenticated attackers to read arbitrary files including credentials and source code. GitLab patched the vulnerability September 10; watchTowr observed exploitation attempts within one day of disclosure. [3]
