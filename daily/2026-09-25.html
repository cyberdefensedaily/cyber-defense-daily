Water Utilities Under Attack, Microsoft Patches Record Flaws

FBI warns of water system intrusions as Microsoft ships largest security update on record

FBI and EPA Warn of Cyberattacks Disrupting U.S. Water Systems

The FBI and EPA issued a public service announcement warning that malicious cyber actors have targeted internet-connected programmable logic controllers at water and wastewater utilities in at least seven states since July 27, with some attacks degrading water operations. Attackers remotely tampered with Rockwell Automation PLCs, changing IP addresses and passwords, resulting in loss of pressure and flooding. The agencies note that pressure loss in drinking water systems could allow untreated groundwater to enter pipes. [1]

Microsoft Patches Record 972 Vulnerabilities, Including Two Exploited Zero-Days

Microsoft's September 2026 Patch Tuesday addressed 972 vulnerabilities, more than double the August count and a new record for the company. The release included fixes for two actively exploited Windows elevation-of-privilege zero-days that allow attackers to gain SYSTEM privileges. The two flaws, CVE-2026-81963 affecting Windows Update Stack and CVE-2026-85880 in Windows ALPC, were both exploited in the wild before patches became available. [2]

Trump Authorizes Private Firms to Conduct Offensive Cyber Operations

President Trump signed a National Security Presidential Memorandum on August 12 authorizing vetted U.S. companies to conduct offensive cyber operations against foreign cyber-enabled transnational criminal organizations under federal control and oversight. The memorandum requires participating companies to sign contracts with the Justice Department or DHS and marks a significant expansion of private sector involvement in activities previously limited to government agencies. The policy builds on Executive Order 14390 from March 2026. [3]

KPMG Survey: AI Security Maturity Improving as Adoption Grows

Businesses are strengthening their AI system security as they gain more experience with the technology, according to KPMG's Q3 2026 AI survey published today. Companies in the experimentation stage were less likely than established users to deploy advanced AI-powered defenses. The report also found accountability improving, with many organizations assigning AI responsibility to C-suite executives or the executive committee. [4]
