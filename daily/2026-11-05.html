Binance Hack, DigiCert Breach, UK AI Warning

Record crypto theft, certificate authority exposure, and infrastructure threat assessment lead today's brief

FBI Attributes $6.3 Billion Binance Hack to North Korea

The FBI confirmed Saturday that North Korean state-sponsored unit TraderTraitor carried out the October 30 theft of $6.3 billion from cryptocurrency exchange Binance, the largest digital-asset heist on record. Binance disclosed Tuesday that hackers used fake LinkedIn recruitment to trick a DevOps engineer into downloading malware that gave attackers access to internal wallet infrastructure. About $3 billion in stolen assets were frozen; Binance says it covered 95% of losses. North Korea denied involvement, calling the allegation a "grave political provocation." [1]

DigiCert Discloses Breach After Misconfiguration Exposed Customer Data

Certificate authority DigiCert confirmed November 5 that unauthorized access to an internal business system exposed archived customer contact details and identity documents including passports and driver's licenses. The breach stemmed from a server migration error in February 2025 that left the system internet-accessible for 17 months; unauthorized access occurred between June 27 and July 2, 2026. DigiCert says its certificate authority services, CertCentral, and DigiCert ONE were unaffected. The company did not disclose how many customers were impacted. [2]

UK NCSC Warns AI-Powered Ransomware Will Hit Infrastructure by 2028

The UK's National Cyber Security Centre assessed in its November 3 annual review that AI-powered malware is "highly likely" to be used in a ransomware attack against critical national infrastructure by 2028. NCSC CEO Richard Horne warned of an "unprecedented rise" in cyber incidents in coming months as China, Russia, and other nation-states expand use of AI-enabled cyber capabilities. The report noted 80 nationally significant cyber attacks in the UK in 2026 and warned that outdated technology exposes essential services to faster, more sophisticated attacks. [3]

Android November Patches Address Over 200 Vulnerabilities

Google released November 2026 Android security updates patching 216 vulnerabilities across two security patch levels. The most severe is CVE-2026-58868, a critical remote code execution flaw in the Framework component requiring no user interaction. The 2026-11-01 patch level resolves 119 defects in Android runtime, Framework, and System; the 2026-11-05 level fixes 97 bugs in Kernel and vendor components from Arm, MediaTek, Unisoc, and Qualcomm.
