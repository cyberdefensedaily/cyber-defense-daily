N-able offline after zero-day dump, SSHole exploited hours after disclosure

Emergency response to 33 disclosed flaws and rapid exploitation defined the weekend.

N-able takes platform offline after researcher dumps 33 zero-days

N-able took its hosted N-central remote management platform offline November 9 after security researcher Sean Bales published proof-of-concept exploits for 33 alleged zero-day vulnerabilities, including authentication bypasses and remote code execution flaws. Bales told BleepingComputer the vulnerabilities were AI-assisted and he bypassed coordinated disclosure after the company allegedly ignored previous vulnerability reports from five to six months ago. N-able said it has not seen active exploitation and is developing a hotfix. [1]

OpenSSH SSHole flaw exploited within hours of November 5 disclosure

Attackers began exploiting CVE-2026-123158, the SSHole vulnerability in OpenSSH, within nine hours of its November 5 disclosure and patch release, according to threat intelligence firm Previdian. The flaw allows remote attackers to log into passwordless accounts on Alpine Linux and other musl-based systems without authentication. Multiple firms confirmed successful exploitation by November 7, with cryptomining and botnet activity observed. [2]

Vanderbilt confirms active cyberattack, orders network shutdown

Vanderbilt University confirmed an active cybersecurity incident November 9 and instructed faculty and staff to disconnect from university Wi-Fi and shut down devices. The ransomware group BYOD claimed responsibility for the breach online. The university is investigating the extent of the compromise. [3]

Democrats won Congress, now eye federal AI regulation

Following Democratic victories in the November 3 midterm elections, tech policy groups are calling on the new Congress to establish federal AI safety standards when it convenes in January 2027. The Chamber of Progress urged lawmakers to require developers to publish safety frameworks and report critical incidents. Voters in at least 29 jurisdictions passed data-center bans or restrictions on election day. [4]
