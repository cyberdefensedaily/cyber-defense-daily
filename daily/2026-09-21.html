Cisco zero-day under attack, tanker cyberattacks expand, North Korean job scam hits 30,000

Maximum-severity ISE flaw actively exploited, maritime incidents mount, fake recruiter campaign disclosed

Cisco patches actively exploited ISE zero-day rated CVSS 10.0

Cisco released emergency fixes for CVE-2026-76460, an authentication bypass in Identity Services Engine that allows unauthenticated remote attackers to gain root access. The company confirmed active exploitation and said it discovered the flaw while working a customer support case, suggesting at least one enterprise was compromised before the vulnerability was known. CISA added it to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on September 16 with a three-day federal patch deadline. No workaround exists. [1]

Third tanker reports suspected cyberattack as US monitors 20 ships

A third tanker crossing the Mediterranean reported internal control system failures, following two confirmed cyberattacks on US-bound oil tankers in August. CISA acknowledged the attacks but said threat actors did not appear to have taken control of the vessels themselves. US authorities are now monitoring at least 20 ships worldwide. Anthropic disclosed that Iran-linked hackers used Claude AI to research US Navy movements and commercial shipboard systems. [2]

North Korean fake recruiters infected 30,000 devices, stole $10.7M

A joint advisory from US, Japanese, Australian, and German authorities disclosed that North Korea's WaterPlum group infected more than 30,000 devices across 100+ countries between December 2025 and July 2026. The hackers posed as recruiters and tricked software developers and IT professionals into running malware during fake technical interviews and coding tests. The operation compromised over 7,000 cryptocurrency wallets and funneled at least $10.71 million to North Korea. [3]
