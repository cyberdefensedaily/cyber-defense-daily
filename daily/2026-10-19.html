Zero-Days, Cloud Ransomware, and AI Agents in the Field

China-linked actors exploit BeyondTrust and Palo Alto flaws; ConnectWise breach hits 173 tenants; Army tests autonomous cyber weapons.

BeyondTrust Zero-Day Exploited Since August by China-Linked Group

BeyondTrust disclosed CVE-2026-102764, a critical vulnerability in Remote Support that China-linked group Armory Castle exploited as a zero-day starting August 29. The flaw, rated 9.3 CVSS, allows unauthenticated attackers to perform arbitrary file operations on appliances that broker privileged remote access. CISA added it to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on October 16 with an October 19 remediation deadline for federal agencies. [1]

Palo Alto Networks Patches PAN-OS Zero-Day Under Active Exploitation

Palo Alto Networks patched CVE-2026-0322, a code injection flaw in PAN-OS management interfaces that allows authenticated attackers to execute arbitrary code with root privileges. The company confirmed limited exploitation and assigned the flaw a CVSS score of 8.6. CISA added the vulnerability to its catalog on October 14 with a two-day federal remediation deadline. [2]

ConnectWise Breach Compromises 173 Microsoft 365 Tenants

ConnectWise suspended its Cloud Backup service on October 17 after confirming hackers stole application credentials and accessed 173 Microsoft 365 customer tenants without passwords or multi-factor authentication. The breach exploited the backup tool's own identity within Microsoft 365 to sign into customer environments. The company disabled seven application identities in its containment response. [3]

Army Cyber Command Developing Autonomous AI Weapons Platform

Documents obtained by The Intercept show Army Cyber Command is building a platform to deploy autonomous AI agents that can navigate and neutralize adversary computer systems. The program follows global concerns about AI agents unexpectedly breaking into systems during testing. ARCYBER conducted soldier touchpoints in October 2026 on prototype systems including man-portable variants. [4]

Japan Cloud Ransomware Attack Hits 495 Organizations

A ransomware attack on IDC Frontier's IDCF Cloud service on October 7 knocked out systems for 495 Japanese companies and local governments. The attackers claim to have encrypted 225 databases containing 3.6 petabytes of data within seven minutes of breaching the East Japan Region 1 infrastructure. IDC Frontier initially said data in four zones might be unrecoverable, but later reported snapshots survived for 194 customers. [5]
