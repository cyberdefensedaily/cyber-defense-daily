Record Binance Breach, Florida Student Data Leak, AI-Powered Attacks

Three major incidents highlight evolving threats and massive exposure of sensitive data.

Binance Reports $6.3 Billion Breach, Largest Crypto Theft on Record

Binance disclosed Thursday that attackers stole approximately $6.3 billion in cryptocurrency after compromising a DevOps laptop, making it the largest crypto heist in history. The exchange has frozen roughly $3 billion in assets and pledged to cover all losses. Blockchain intelligence firm TRM Labs attributed the attack to North Korean state-sponsored hackers, citing tradecraft similarities to earlier thefts. [1]

Florida Student Aid Breach Exposes 4.9 Million Social Security Numbers

The Florida Department of Education confirmed October 29 that unauthorized access to its student financial aid website may have exposed Social Security numbers and other personal information of up to 4.9 million people. The department detected the breach October 19 and is working with the Florida Department of Law Enforcement on a criminal investigation. Current financial aid disbursements are not affected. [2]

Chainalysis Confirms Breach Using 'Offensive Agentic Techniques'

Blockchain analytics firm Chainalysis disclosed today that attackers used what it describes as 'offensive agentic techniques'—AI-powered attack methods—to access internal systems and expose customer names, email addresses, and account roles. The company says no evidence indicates passwords, authentication tokens, or sensitive investigation data were compromised. Chainalysis emphasized the incident is unrelated to the Binance theft it is tracking. [3]

CISA Adds Ivanti Connect Secure Flaws to Exploited Vulnerabilities List

The Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency added two Ivanti Connect Secure vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog October 30, citing evidence of active exploitation. The flaws—CVE-2026-111357 and CVE-2026-118527—involve missing authentication and predictable identifier generation. [4]

Federal Election Commission Filing System Disrupted by Cyberattack Days Before Midterms

The FEC restored its electronic filing system October 28 after a cyberattack knocked it offline for roughly 41 hours beginning Monday evening, just days before the November 3 midterm elections. The agency temporarily disabled internet-facing services after detecting an attempted breach. The identity of the attacker and the method used remain undisclosed. [5]
